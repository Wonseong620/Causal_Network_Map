About

This viewer shows lead–lag networks among 50 ICIO V1 sectors, estimated from news about the 2026 Iran war in four language corpora (Arabic, Chinese, English, Persian; 58,530 items, January–April 2026). It accompanies the paper Qualitative Shock Mapping for Quantitative Production Network Models: Evidence from Iran-Related Hostilities (Kim & Erokhin, IIASA).

Paper link: forthcoming.

Method

  1. Collect. News items on the hostilities are gathered per language stratum with publication date, source, and risk metadata.
  2. Map to sectors. Each article receives a probability vector over the 50 ICIO V1 sectors (lexical TF-IDF classifier; a zero-shot LLM classifier is used for robustness). Direct industry evidence takes priority; purely political items map mainly to public administration and defence.
  3. Aggregate daily. Article vectors are summed per day and language, weighted by negativity and war-relatedness, then transformed to within-day shares; low-information (near-uniform) articles are excluded.
  4. Test lead–lag pairs. All 2,450 ordered sector pairs are tested with Toda–Yamamoto lag-augmented Granger tests on the daily series.
  5. Select edges. Pairs are kept if they pass Benjamini–Hochberg false-discovery-rate control at 5%; each edge is annotated with its lead (1–7 days) and the lagged Pearson correlation.
  6. Draw. Sectors sit on a fixed ring (V1 1 at the top, clockwise), sized by total exposure and coloured by sector group; edges are shaded by lead and dashed when the correlation is negative.
